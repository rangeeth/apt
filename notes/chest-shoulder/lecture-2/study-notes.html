Chest Shoulder Demo: Study Notes

Detailed notesStudy notesMarkdown
Study notes written from the lecture recording and the course slides. They may contain mistakes: check the “Points to Confirm” section and verify against the official course material.

Course: Personal Training Certification

Topic: Bench presses, cable and machine presses, flies, overhead presses, lateral raises, upright row, posterior deltoid exercises, the multi-flight machine and rotator cuff exercises

About these notes: Short revision version of the Chest Shoulder Demo. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The muscle theory is in the Chest Shoulder Lecture notes.

Big idea: The bar path is straight, and the set-up (leg drive, scapular position, bench angle, equipment choice) decides which muscle works and how well.

1. Bench press basics

  • "Bench press" alone means a flat bench with a barbell. With a dumbbell it is a dumbbell chest press.
  • Three sources of power: muscle contraction, the stretch reflex (eccentric first) and leg drive (press the floor, force travels up the kinetic chain).
  • The arch is a result of leg drive and is not built on purpose.
  • Keep both feet on the floor for a wide base of support.

2. Barbell set-up

  • Rack height: bar at wrist level or just below with the arms extended.
  • Hand distance: forearms vertical at the bottom of the lift.
  • Spotter behind the head.
  • Valsalva: inhale, lower, press, exhale at the end of the repetition.

3. Dumbbell, decline and incline

  • Dumbbell: swing the dumbbells up from the thighs to start. Upper arm points to the sternum.
  • Decline: knees and ankles press on two roller pads for leg drive. Bar path stays straight.
  • Incline: do not sit on the seat, lean back in a mini squat. Keep the head off the bench and steady.

4. Scapulae

  • Retract and depress the scapulae before the first repetition.
  • It protects the shoulder, points the humerus at the right place and gives each person a personal landing point.

5. Cable and machine presses

  • Cable press on a PT station, with the cables close so the path is straight. One leg forward keeps the centre of gravity inside the base of support.
  • Machine presses: the lever path decides the angle. Look at the direction of the humerus to see which head works.

6. Flies

  • Free weight: high load when the muscle is long, almost none at the top. Going heavy turns it into a press.
  • Cable: low pulley incline scoop gives a better resistance profile.
  • Cam machine (pec deck): eccentric first for the stretch reflex, elbows shy of lock, range limiting device protects the range.

7. Overhead press

  • Strict: no help from the legs.
  • Push press: dip, drive (spring), the deltoid finishes, dip again to absorb the landing. Structural lift with a barbell.
  • Push jerk: drop under the bar in a split stance. Heaviest of all.
  • Seated press: back rest at about 80 degrees, not 90.

8. Shoulder isolation and compound

  • Lateral raise: no shrugging, stay in the frontal plane. Cable from behind the body trains the supraspinatus too.
  • Upright row: compound for the middle deltoid.
  • Posterior deltoid: reverse fly (concentric first), chest supported face pull, rear delt row, prone high row (elbows out).
  • Standing multi-flight machine: anterior and middle deltoid, and an isolation exercise for shoulder adduction (latissimus dorsi).

9. Rotator cuff exercises

  • Cable level with the forearm so the line of force matches the horizontal movement.
  • Side-lying dumbbell external rotation: line of force matches, lifting phase only.

10. Key terms

12. Revision questions

  1. What does "bench press" mean on its own?
  2. Name the three sources of power in a bench press.
  3. What is the arch a sign of?
  4. Where should the bar be on the rack?
  5. How do you find the right hand distance?
  6. How is leg drive created in a decline press?
  7. Why does the lifter not sit on the seat of an incline bench?
  8. Why keep the head off the bench?
  9. What do scapular retraction and depression do?
  10. Why does one leg go forward in a cable chest press?
  11. Why does a free weight fly turn into a press when heavy?
  12. What is a range limiting device?
  13. What is the difference between a strict press and a push press?
  14. What is the correct back rest angle for a seated overhead press?
  15. Why is the cable lateral raise done from behind the body?

Answers

  1. A flat bench with a barbell.
  2. Muscle contraction, the stretch reflex and leg drive.
  3. A sign that the lifter is pressing the floor (leg drive).
  4. At wrist level or slightly below, with the arms extended.
  5. Lower the bar to the chest and pause: the forearms should be vertical.
  6. By pressing the knees and ankles against the two roller pads.
  7. A 90 degree hip and knee wastes force. A mini squat lets the force flow upward.
  8. The bench pushes the head back and stresses the neck.
  9. They place the shoulder joints safely, point the humerus correctly and set a personal landing point.
  10. It widens the base of support so the centre of gravity stays inside it.
  11. Because the path is not restricted, the body bends the elbows and presses.
  12. A latch on the machine that limits the range of the stretch.
  13. The strict press has no help from the legs. The push press uses a dip and drive from the hips and knees.
  14. About 80 degrees.
  15. The body blocks the arm from leaving the frontal plane, and the cable gives resistance from the start.
