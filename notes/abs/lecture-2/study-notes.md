# Abs Demo: Study Notes

**Course:** Personal Training Certification

**Topic:** Crunches, reverse crunches, torso rotation, lateral flexion, planks and the advanced schedule

**About these notes:** Short revision version of the Abs Demo. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The muscle theory is in the Abs Lecture notes.

**Big idea:** Set up the exercise so only the intended joint moves, and use the moment arm to make it easier or harder.

---

## 1. Forward crunch

- Knees bent, elbows to the ceiling, head and shoulders up. Do not touch elbows to knees (it becomes a sit-up).
- **Breathing:** exhale on the concentric, inhale on the eccentric. No Valsalva.
- **Easy to hard:** incline, flat, decline, inverted.
- **Lat pulldown crunch:** face away, handle behind the neck (or a sling) so the lats cannot help.
- **Crunch cum sit-up:** spine flexion (rectus abdominis) then hip flexion (iliopsoas).

## 2. Reverse crunch

- Goal: spine flexion with no hip flexion.
- Start: hip pre-flexed, **knee locked**. Hamstrings are lengthened, so **muscle passive insufficiency** blocks more hip flexion.
- The angle differs for each person. Legs flat on the floor is wrong (it is a leg raise).
- Harder: incline (30 to 45 degrees), then hanging.

## 3. Rotation

- Russian twist: vertical load, horizontal rotation, so a cable is better.
- **Seated cable twist:** seated (stable pelvis), handle held in the far hand and the other hand holds the elbow (cam effect, no arm pulling). A Swiss ball completes the cam.
- **Twisting crunch cum sit-up:** start the rotation from the floor. Do one side then the other, not alternate, so the rectus abdominis and iliopsoas rest.

## 4. Lateral flexion

- Weight must go **up** in the concentric. One dumbbell on the **opposite** side. Two dumbbells balance out.
- **Best:** seated high pulley, arm over the head (long moment arm, no lat help).
- **Back extension bench sideways:** only go up, pad below the hip, hug a plate.

## 5. Planks

- Straight line, neutral neck, upper arm at 90 degrees, feet at 90 degrees.
- **Harder:** lift a leg, then a hand too, unstable ball, side plank, side plank with arm and leg abducted.
- **Easier:** knees down, side plank with feet crossed.

## 6. Advanced schedule (6 to 8 RM)

1. Forward crunch (machine, decline, inverted) or reverse crunch (incline, ab dip stand, hanging).
2. High pulley lateral flexion.
3. Seated trunk rotation (machine or cable).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Moment arm | Distance from the fulcrum to the line of force |
| Muscle passive insufficiency | A lengthened biarticular muscle cannot lengthen more at the other joint |
| Cam effect | The body or ball wraps the cable, which varies the resistance |
| Isometric | Muscle length stays the same while tension changes |

## Where the sources differ

- The slides list the exercises without the set-up reasons. The class gave the reasons (seated, arm over the head, hip pre-flexed).

## Revision questions

1. Why exhale on the concentric in a crunch?
2. How do you make a forward crunch harder?
3. Why use a lat pulldown machine for crunches facing away?
4. Why does the reverse crunch start with the hip pre-flexed and knee locked?
5. Why is a cable better than a dumbbell for rotation?
6. Why is the seated cable twist done seated?
7. In a dumbbell side bend, which side is the dumbbell held on?
8. Why is the arm put over the head in high pulley lateral flexion?
9. Name two ways to make a plank harder and two to make a side plank easier.
10. What is the advanced schedule?

## Answers

1. Valsalva would compress the front of the spine to keep it straight. A crunch needs the spine to flex.
2. Move to a decline or inverted position (longer moment arm).
3. Shoulder extension is blocked, so only spine flexion remains.
4. Muscle passive insufficiency of the hamstrings stops further hip flexion, so only the spine moves.
5. A cable gives a horizontal line of force that matches the horizontal rotation.
6. The pelvis stays still, so only the torso rotates.
7. The opposite side, so the weight rises when the muscles contract.
8. It lengthens the moment arm and locks the arm so the latissimus dorsi cannot help.
9. Harder: lift a leg or hand, unstable surface. Easier: knees down, feet crossed.
10. Crunch (forward or reverse), high pulley lateral flexion, seated trunk rotation, at 6 to 8 RM.
