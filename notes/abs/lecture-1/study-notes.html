Abs Lecture: Study Notes

Detailed notesStudy notesMarkdown
Study notes written from the lecture recording and the course slides. They may contain mistakes: check the “Points to Confirm” section and verify against the official course material.

Course: Personal Training Certification

Topic: The core, the eight abdominal muscles, rectus abdominis and the six-pack, hip flexors, obliques, transversus abdominis, layers and quadratus lumborum

About these notes: Short revision version of the Abs Lecture. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The exercises are in the Abs Demo notes.

Big idea: Decide the prime mover by the joint action and the fulcrum, not by where the exercise is felt.

1. Core and the eight muscles

  • Core = paraspinal muscles + gluteal group + abdominal muscles.
  • Front wall: rectus abdominis, external obliques, internal obliques, transversus abdominis.
  • Back wall: iliacus and psoas major (hip flexors, together iliopsoas), psoas minor, quadratus lumborum.

2. Rectus abdominis

  • Origin: pubic symphysis. Insertion: costal cartilages 5 to 7 and the xiphoid process.
  • Straight fibres. Linea alba is the vertical white line, tendinous intersections are the horizontal bands.
  • Function: spine flexion (two ways: forward crunch or reverse crunch).
  • Packs are genetic: 1 intersection gives 4 packs, 2 gives 6, 3 gives 8. Uneven packs come from unaligned intersections.

4. Back wall

  • Iliacus: iliac fossa to the lesser trochanter. Psoas major: T12-L5 to the same lesser trochanter. Both cross the hip: hip flexion.
  • Psoas minor: T12-L1 to the pelvis. Does not cross the hip, so it is not a hip flexor. Weak spine flexion.
  • QL: iliac crest to L1-L5 and the last rib. Spine extension (synergist), lateral flexion (synergist) and stabilisation. Never a prime mover.

5. Leg raise: role of the rectus abdominis

Stabiliser. The iliopsoas pulls the pelvis into anterior tilt, which causes hyperlordosis. The rectus abdominis holds the pelvis neutral.

6. Obliques and TA

MuscleFibresRotationLateral flexion
EODown and inwardOpposite sideSame side
IOUp and inwardSame sideSame side
  • To rotate right: left EO + right IO.
  • TA: innermost belt. Functions: bracing (spine stabilisation) and abdominal compression (helps the diaphragm return in exhalation).
  • Layers: outer EO, middle IO and RA, inner TA.

7. Muscle passive insufficiency

A multiarticular muscle already lengthened at one joint cannot lengthen further at the other.

Where the sources differ

  • The slide lists the psoas minor function as "weak trunk flexion". The class called it "weak spine flexion".

Revision questions

  1. What three groups form the core?
  2. What decides the number of packs?
  3. Where does the rectus abdominis start and end?
  4. Which exercises in the four-exercise board are hip flexion?
  5. Why is the psoas minor not a hip flexor?
  6. What is the role of the rectus abdominis in a leg raise?
  7. Which direction do EO fibres run?
  8. To rotate to the right, which obliques work?
  9. What are the functions of the TA?
  10. In which layer are the RA and IO?
  11. What are the QL functions?

Answers

  1. Paraspinal muscles, gluteal group and abdominal muscles.
  2. The number of tendinous intersections a person has (genetics).
  3. Pubic symphysis to the costal cartilages 5 to 7 and the xiphoid process.
  4. Sit-up and leg raise.
  5. It ends on the pelvis, not the femur, so it does not cross the hip.
  6. Stabiliser: it holds the pelvis neutral against anterior pelvic tilt.
  7. Down and inward.
  8. The left EO and the right IO.
  9. Spine stabilisation (bracing) and abdominal compression.
  10. The middle layer.
  11. Spine extension (synergist), lateral flexion (synergist) and stabilisation.
