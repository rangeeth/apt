# Arms Lecture: Study Notes

**Course:** Personal Training Certification

**Topic:** Upper arm and forearm muscle groups, joints crossed, the grip ranking table, the triceps long head and the forearm muscles

**About these notes:** Short revision version of the Arms Lecture. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The exercises are in the Arms Demo notes.

**Big idea:** Anterior muscles flex and posterior muscles extend. The number of joints a muscle crosses and the grip or shoulder position decide which part of the arm works.

---

## 1. Four groups

| | Anterior | Posterior |
|---|---|---|
| Upper arm | Elbow flexors | Elbow extensors |
| Forearm | Wrist flexors | Wrist extensors |

## 2. Elbow flexors

- **Biceps brachii:** long head (supraglenoid tubercle) and short head (coracoid process) to the radial tuberosity. Crosses **3 joints**: shoulder flexion, elbow flexion and supination.
- **Brachialis:** lower half of the front of the humerus to the ulnar tuberosity. 1 joint, elbow flexion.
- **Brachioradialis:** supracondylar ridge to the styloid process of the radius, side to side. 1 joint, elbow flexion.

## 3. Grip ranking (1 = leads)

| | Supinated | Pronated | Mid-prone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biceps | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| Brachialis | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| Brachioradialis | 3 | 2 | 1 |

- Pronation relaxes the biceps by **reciprocal inhibition**.
- The brachialis inserts on the ulna, which does not rotate, so the grip does not affect it.
- Exercise match: biceps, dumbbell curl with supination. Brachialis, reverse curl. Brachioradialis, hammer curl.

## 4. Triceps brachii

- Three heads, one tendon to the **olecranon**.
- **Long head:** infraglenoid tubercle. Crosses the shoulder and elbow (biarticular). Elbow extension plus shoulder extension.
- **Lateral head:** above the radial groove. **Medial head:** below it. Elbow only.
- The radial groove is named for the radial nerve.

## 5. Long head problem areas

| Type | Cause | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | MAI: shoulder extensors already held contracted | Kickback |
| 2A | RI, compound: shoulder flexes | Close grip bench, dips, pushdown |
| 2B | RI, isolation: shoulder held flexed | Skull crusher, overhead extension |

- **Best exercise:** standing cable triceps extension (arms by the sides). No RI, no MAI, and the long head is strongest at zero degrees of shoulder elevation.
- Dips: upright body for the triceps, leaning forward for chest and shoulder.

## 6. Forearm names

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carpi | Wrist bones |
| Radialis / ulnaris | Thumb side / little finger side |
| Digitorum | Fingers 2 to 5 |
| Pollicis | Thumb |
| Palmaris | Palm (no palmaris on the back) |
| Superficialis / profundus | Surface / deep |
| Longus / brevis | Long / short |

- **Wrist flexors (6):** origin mostly the medial epicondyle. Functions: wrist flexion and **gripping**.
- **Wrist extensors (5):** origin mostly the lateral epicondyle. Function: wrist extension.
- Superficialis ends on the middle phalanx and profundus on the distal phalanx.

## 7. Principles

- Train the prime function. MAI and RI limit multiarticular muscles.
- Match the resistance profile to the strength profile (cable curl facing away).
- Change of working muscle mid-repetition gives neither muscle a full repetition.
- Frequency: elbow flexors work in rows and pull downs, triceps in presses.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Uni-, bi-, triarticular | Crosses one, two or three joints |
| MAI | A muscle already contracted at one joint cannot contract fully at the other |
| RI | The opposite muscle relaxes when one contracts |
| Aponeurosis | A sheet that works like a tendon |
| Phalanx | Finger bone: proximal, middle, distal |

## Where the sources differ

- The slides list the biceps functions as elbow flexion, supination and shoulder flexion. The class stressed that elbow flexion is the target.
- The slides list the triceps long head with weak shoulder extension and adduction. The class discussed extension only.

## Revision questions

1. Which muscle group is posterior in the forearm?
2. How many joints does the biceps cross, and which?
3. Why is the brachialis unaffected by grip?
4. Which grip ranks the biceps lowest, and why?
5. Which exercise matches each rank 1 grip?
6. Where do the three triceps heads start?
7. Which triceps head is biarticular?
8. What are problem areas 1, 2A and 2B?
9. Why is the cable triceps extension recommended?
10. What does digitorum mean? What does pollicis mean?
11. Where do the flexor digitorum superficialis and profundus end?
12. What is the main function to train in the forearm flexors?

## Answers

1. The wrist extensors.
2. Three: shoulder, elbow and radioulnar.
3. It inserts on the ulna, which does not move in pronation or supination.
4. Pronated (rank 3), because pronation relaxes the supinator side (reciprocal inhibition).
5. Biceps: dumbbell curl with supination. Brachialis: reverse curl. Brachioradialis: hammer curl.
6. Long head: infraglenoid tubercle. Lateral: above the radial groove. Medial: below it.
7. The long head.
8. 1 is MAI (kickback). 2A is RI in compound lifts. 2B is RI in isolation lifts with the shoulder held flexed.
9. The arms stay by the sides, so no RI and no MAI, and the long head is strongest there.
10. Digitorum is fingers 2 to 5. Pollicis is the thumb.
11. Superficialis on the middle phalanx and profundus on the distal phalanx.
12. Gripping.
