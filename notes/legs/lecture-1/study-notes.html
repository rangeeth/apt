Legs Lecture: Study Notes

Detailed notesStudy notesMarkdown
Study notes written from the lecture recording and the course slides. They may contain mistakes: check the “Points to Confirm” section and verify against the official course material.

Course: Personal Training Certification

Topic: Why train the legs, joints and actions, quadriceps, reciprocal inhibition and the leg extension, hamstrings, gluteal group, adductors, sartorius and the lower leg

About these notes: Short revision version of the Legs Lecture. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The exercise demonstrations are in the Legs Demo notes.

Big idea: For each muscle learn the name, the function (the joint action) and an exercise whose concentric phase is that function. Whether a muscle crosses one joint or two decides how to train it.

1. Why train the legs

  • Power generation: the largest muscles in the body.
  • Daily tasks: standing, stairs, walking, lifting from the floor.
  • Sport: nearly every sport needs leg strength.

2. Joints and actions

JointTypeActions
HipBall and socketFlexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotations
KneeHingeFlexion, extension
AnkleHingePlantarflexion, dorsiflexion

The hip, knee and ankle are the three load bearing joints.

3. Quadriceps femoris

  • Quadri is four, ceps is heads, femoris is of the femur. Rectus is straight. Vasti is the plural of vastus.
  • Rectus femoris: starts on the AIIS, crosses hip and knee (biarticular). Knee extension and hip flexion.
  • Vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius: start on the femur, cross the knee only (uniarticular). Knee extension.
  • All four insert on the tibial tuberosity through the patellar ligament.
  • Medius means medium. Medialis means towards the midline.

4. Compound and isolation

  • Compound exercises for the quadriceps: squat, regular and sumo deadlift, leg press, lunges, step-ups.
  • Reciprocal inhibition: when the hip extensors work, the rectus femoris (a hip flexor) is inhibited, so compound movements under-train it.
  • Active insufficiency: on an upright leg extension the hip is already flexed, so the rectus femoris is already short.
  • Fix: recline the seat back to about 40 degrees. A study showed much more rectus femoris growth than upright.

5. Hamstrings

  • Biceps femoris (long and short head), semitendinosus, semimembranosus.
  • Knee flexion. All but the short head also extend the hip.
  • Exercises: leg curl, stiff leg and Romanian deadlift.

6. Gluteal group, adductors, sartorius

  • Gluteus maximus: hip extension and external rotation (hip thrust, deadlifts, squats).
  • Gluteus medius and minimus: hip abduction and pelvic stability (standing cable abduction).
  • Adductors: hip adduction, and the magnus also extends the hip (seated and standing adduction).
  • Sartorius: longest muscle, a stabiliser (hip flexion, abduction, external rotation, knee flexion).

7. Lower leg

  • Gastrocnemius: crosses knee and ankle. Plantarflexion, helps knee flexion. Works best with the knee straight.
  • Soleus: crosses the ankle only. Plantarflexion. Does most of the work with the knee bent.
  • Tibialis anterior: dorsiflexion and inversion (toe raise).
  • Both calf muscles share the Achilles tendon.

9. Where the sources differ

  • Hamstrings, gluteals, adductors and sartorius: no recording was available, so these follow the slides and the board photos.
  • Gluteus medius and minimus: the slides list abduction and internal rotation, and the class stressed the lateral hip stabiliser role.

10. Revision questions

  1. Why is the rectus femoris biarticular?
  2. Which muscle group has the plural name vasti, and what do they do?
  3. Where do all four quadriceps muscles insert?
  4. What does the word medius mean, and how is it different from medialis?
  5. Why do compound exercises under-train the rectus femoris?
  6. What is active insufficiency?
  7. How should the leg extension seat be set to train the rectus femoris?
  8. Which hamstring head crosses the knee only?
  9. What are the functions of the gluteus maximus?
  10. What does the gluteus medius do?
  11. Name the main adductor that also extends the hip.
  12. Why does the gastrocnemius work less in a seated calf raise?
  13. Which muscle does most of the work in a seated calf raise?
  14. Which muscle dorsiflexes the ankle?
  15. Which tendon do the gastrocnemius and soleus share?

Answers

  1. It starts on the pelvis (AIIS) and inserts below the knee, so it crosses the hip and the knee.
  2. The vastus lateralis, medialis and intermedius. They extend the knee.
  3. The tibial tuberosity, through the patellar ligament.
  4. Medius means medium (in the middle). Medialis means towards the midline.
  5. The hip extensors contract and inhibit the rectus femoris, a hip flexor, so only the vasti do the work.
  6. A muscle that is already shortened across one joint cannot produce much force across the other.
  7. Reclined to about 40 degrees, so the hip is open.
  8. The short head of the biceps femoris.
  9. Hip extension and external rotation.
  10. Hip abduction and stabilising the pelvis from side to side.
  11. The adductor magnus.
  12. It is already shortened at the knee, so it cannot contribute much to ankle plantarflexion.
  13. The soleus.
  14. The tibialis anterior.
  15. The Achilles tendon.
