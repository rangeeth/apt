Legs Demo: Study Notes

Detailed notesStudy notesMarkdown
Study notes written from the lecture recording and the course slides. They may contain mistakes: check the “Points to Confirm” section and verify against the official course material.

Course: Personal Training Certification

Topic: Squats and lunges, supported squats, step-ups, the leg press, stiff leg and Romanian deadlifts, hip isolation, leg extension, leg curls, calf and tibialis exercises, abduction and adduction

About these notes: Short revision version of the Legs Demo. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The muscle theory is in the Legs Lecture notes.

Big idea: Choose the exercise by the joint action, then adjust the set-up (foot position, seat angle, order) to change which muscle does the work.

1. Why squats and lunges

  • Squat: functional carryover (mimics sitting, trains hip, knee and ankle mobility) and structural development (soft tissue, bone density).
  • Lunge: unilateral and symmetrical development, lateral hip stability (gluteus medius and minimus), balance.

2. Squats

  • Back squat: bar on the widest part of the upper back, hands close, knees follow the toes, gaze on a focal point.
  • Front squat: front rack, elbows high, two-finger grip. Carries over to the clean and jerk.
  • Overhead squat: wide grip, higher centre of gravity, less stable.
  • Power cage is safer than a squat rack because of the safety arms.

3. Resistance profile and supported squats

  • The squat has two moment arms, at the hip and at the knee.
  • Smith machine: feet forward shifts work to the gluteals and hamstrings, feet under the bar shifts it to the quadriceps.
  • Hack squat, V-squat, pendulum squat, belt squat, Smith machine and ball squat give a fixed path.

4. Lunges and step-ups

  • Static: the front leg works. Dynamic: plyometric, stabiliser then prime mover then stabiliser. Walking: eccentric like dynamic, concentric like static. Reverse: the working leg stays still, a step towards step-ups.
  • Step-up: box about 3 feet, wall beside it, raised heel helps dorsiflexion.
  • Pistol squat: a deep single leg squat.

5. Leg press

  • Vertical (back stress), horizontal (moment arm shifts to the hip), 45 degrees (balanced).
  • High platform: more hip extensors. Low platform: more quadriceps.
  • Head on the pad. Toes out for two legs, toes straight for one leg.

6. Deadlift variations and hip isolation

  • SLDL: deloading, concentric first. RDL: non-deloading, eccentric first, stretch reflex helps.
  • Hip extension relay: adductor magnus (bottom), hamstrings (mid), gluteus maximus (top).
  • Hip thrust, cable pull-through and glute kickback isolate the gluteus maximus.

7. Knee and ankle exercises

  • Leg extension: recline the seat so the rectus femoris works.
  • Standing calf raise (gastrocnemius and soleus), seated calf raise (soleus), calf press, toe raise (tibialis anterior).
  • Order: the gastrocnemius helps in the leg curl, so do the standing calf raise before the seated leg curl.

8. Abduction and adduction

  • Seated machines work in the horizontal plane.
  • Standing cable versions work in the frontal plane: abduction for the gluteus medius and minimus, adduction for the adductors.

9. Key terms

TermMeaning

Answers

  1. It mimics sitting and standing and trains hip, knee and ankle mobility together.
  2. Single leg symmetry and lateral hip stability.
  3. On the widest area of the upper back, along the spine of the scapula.
  4. The safety arms catch the bar, so heavy work is safer.
  5. The bar rests on the front of the shoulders with the elbows high.
  6. More work moves to the gluteals and hamstrings.
  7. The reverse lunge.
  8. The resting leg can hang without touching the floor, giving a greater range.
  9. The vertical press stressed the lower back, and the horizontal one removed that.
  10. It uses more of the hip extensors.
  11. The SLDL is deloading and starts with the lift. The RDL is non-deloading and starts with the lowering.
  12. Adductor magnus, then hamstrings, then gluteus maximus.
  13. Reclined to about 40 degrees.
  14. The seated calf raise.
  15. The gastrocnemius helps in the leg curl, so the curl would tire it first.
