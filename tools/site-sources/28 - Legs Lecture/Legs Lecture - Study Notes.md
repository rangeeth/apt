# Legs Lecture: Study Notes

**Course:** Personal Training Certification

**Topic:** Why train the legs, joints and actions, quadriceps, reciprocal inhibition and the leg extension, hamstrings, gluteal group, adductors, sartorius and the lower leg

**About these notes:** Short revision version of the Legs Lecture. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The exercise demonstrations are in the Legs Demo notes.

**Big idea:** For each muscle learn the name, the function (the joint action) and an exercise whose concentric phase is that function. Whether a muscle crosses one joint or two decides how to train it.

---

## 1. Why train the legs

- **Power generation:** the largest muscles in the body.
- **Daily tasks:** standing, stairs, walking, lifting from the floor.
- **Sport:** nearly every sport needs leg strength.

## 2. Joints and actions

| Joint | Type | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Hip | Ball and socket | Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotations |
| Knee | Hinge | Flexion, extension |
| Ankle | Hinge | Plantarflexion, dorsiflexion |

The hip, knee and ankle are the three load bearing joints.

## 3. Quadriceps femoris

- Quadri is four, ceps is heads, femoris is of the femur. Rectus is straight. Vasti is the plural of vastus.
- **Rectus femoris:** starts on the AIIS, crosses hip and knee (biarticular). Knee extension and hip flexion.
- **Vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius:** start on the femur, cross the knee only (uniarticular). Knee extension.
- All four insert on the tibial tuberosity through the patellar ligament.
- Medius means medium. Medialis means towards the midline.

## 4. Compound and isolation

- Compound exercises for the quadriceps: squat, regular and sumo deadlift, leg press, lunges, step-ups.
- **Reciprocal inhibition:** when the hip extensors work, the rectus femoris (a hip flexor) is inhibited, so compound movements under-train it.
- **Active insufficiency:** on an upright leg extension the hip is already flexed, so the rectus femoris is already short.
- **Fix:** recline the seat back to about 40 degrees. A study showed much more rectus femoris growth than upright.

## 5. Hamstrings

- Biceps femoris (long head, short head), semitendinosus, semimembranosus. Origin: ischial tuberosity (the short head: linea aspera).
- **Long head, semitendinosus, semimembranosus:** biarticular (hip and knee). **Short head:** knee only.
- Functions: knee flexion (all), hip extension (the three biarticular members).
- **Compound problem:** in squats, deadlifts, leg press and lunges the knee extends, so the knee flexors (hamstrings) relax. This is reciprocal inhibition, so the hamstrings need **isolation**.
- **Isolation:** stiff leg and Romanian deadlifts (hip extension, knee stays extended) and the leg curl (knee flexion). The leg curl is the only exercise for the short head.
- **Leg curl type:** lying flat (hip extended, already contracted), angled bench (better), **seated** (hamstrings lengthened at the hip, best).

## 6. Gluteal group, adductors, sartorius

- **Gluteus maximus** (the largest muscle): hip extension, external rotation, horizontal abduction. Hip thrust, kickback, cable pull through, deadlifts and squats.
- **Gluteus medius and minimus:** abduction, rotation (both ways) and **lateral stabilisation of the hip** (isometric). Standing cable abduction. Stabilisation needs **unilateral and unsupported** exercises (lunges, step-ups, pistol squats), not a unilateral leg press.
- **Adductors (magnus, longus, brevis):** hip adduction. The magnus also has vertical fibres to the medial condyle of the femur and extends the hip. Cable and machine adduction, sumo deadlift and squat.
- **Hip extension relay:** adductor magnus at the bottom, hamstrings in the middle, gluteus maximus at the top.
- **Sartorius:** the longest muscle, independent, a stabiliser. Hip flexion, abduction, external rotation and knee flexion together make the cross-legged tailor position.

## 7. Lower leg

- **Gastrocnemius:** crosses knee and ankle. Plantarflexion, helps knee flexion. Works best with the knee straight.
- **Soleus:** crosses the ankle only. Plantarflexion. Does most of the work with the knee bent.
- **Tibialis anterior:** dorsiflexion and inversion (toe raise).
- Both calf muscles share the Achilles tendon.

## 8. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Biarticular | Crosses two joints |
| Uniarticular | Crosses one joint |
| Reciprocal inhibition | The nervous system relaxes the opposite muscle when one contracts |
| Active insufficiency | A muscle that is already short produces little force |
| Plantarflexion | Toes point down |
| Dorsiflexion | Toes lift towards the shin |
| Tuberosity | A large rough bump on a bone |
| Linea aspera | The rough line along the back of the femur |

## 9. Where the sources differ

- **Gluteus medius and minimus:** the slides list abduction and internal rotation. The class gave abduction, rotation both ways and lateral stabilisation of the hip.

## Answers

1. It starts on the pelvis (AIIS) and inserts below the knee, so it crosses the hip and the knee.
2. The vastus lateralis, medialis and intermedius. They extend the knee.
3. The tibial tuberosity, through the patellar ligament.
4. Medius means medium (in the middle). Medialis means towards the midline.
5. The hip extensors contract and inhibit the rectus femoris, a hip flexor, so only the vasti do the work.
6. A muscle that is already shortened across one joint cannot produce much force across the other.
7. Reclined to about 40 degrees, so the hip is open.
8. The short head of the biceps femoris. The leg curl is the only exercise that trains it.
9. Hip extension, external rotation and horizontal abduction.
10. Hip abduction, rotation and lateral stabilisation of the hip. Unilateral, unsupported exercises (lunges, step-ups, pistol squats).
11. The adductor magnus.
12. It is already shortened at the knee, so it cannot contribute much to ankle plantarflexion.
13. The soleus.
14. The tibialis anterior.
15. The Achilles tendon.
16. Reciprocal inhibition: the knee extends, so the hamstrings relax. The seated leg curl is best.
17. Adductor magnus (bottom), hamstrings (middle), gluteus maximus (top).
18. The longest muscle, independent, a leg stabiliser (hip flexion, abduction, external rotation, knee flexion).
