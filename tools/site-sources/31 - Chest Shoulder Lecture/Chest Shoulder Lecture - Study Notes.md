# Chest Shoulder Lecture: Study Notes

**Course:** Personal Training Certification

**Topic:** Why train chest and shoulder, the glenohumeral joint, rotator cuff group, deltoids, pectoralis major heads, bench angles and the pectoralis minor

**About these notes:** Short revision version of the Chest Shoulder Lecture. For the full explanation, see the Detailed Notes. The exercise demonstrations are in the Chest Shoulder Demo notes.

**Big idea:** The shoulder is the most mobile and least stable joint, so it has two layers of muscle round it. Learn each muscle's function, then pick the exercise whose concentric phase is that function. For the chest, the bench angle decides which head leads.

---

## 1. Why train chest and shoulder

- **Pushing strength:** horizontal push and vertical push.
- **Protection:** the muscles shield the shoulder joint.

## 2. The glenohumeral joint

- Ball and socket: head of the humerus in the shallow glenoid cavity of the scapula.
- **Less structurally stable** (shallow socket, so dislocation is more common than at the hip), **highly mobile** (11 joint actions, the extra one is vertical abduction) and **widely used**.

## 3. Two layers

- **Deep:** rotator cuff group.
- **Superficial:** deltoids.

## 4. Rotator cuff group

| Muscle | Attaches to | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Supraspinatus | Top of the greater tubercle | Initial few degrees (20 to 30) of abduction |
| Infraspinatus | Middle of the greater tubercle | External rotation |
| Teres minor | Bottom of the greater tubercle | External rotation |
| Subscapularis | Lesser tubercle | Internal rotation |

- Common function: hold the head of the humerus in the socket (prime mover for the standalone jobs, stabiliser for the cuff job).
- Train them for strength at the **end** of the workout, for mobility at the **start**.
- Use a **cable** level with the forearm so the line of force matches the horizontal movement.

## 5. Deltoids

| Part | Origin | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior | Lateral half of the clavicle | Flexion, vertical abduction, horizontal adduction, internal rotation |
| Middle | Acromion | Abduction |
| Posterior | Spine of the scapula | Extension, adduction, horizontal abduction, external rotation |

- All three insert on the deltoid tuberosity. The posterior deltoid is the antagonist of the anterior deltoid.
- Do not say medial. The middle part is between the other two.

## 6. Deltoid exercises

- **Anterior:** front raise (flexion) and overhead press (vertical abduction: strict, push press, jerk). Synergist in horizontal adduction and internal rotation.
- **Middle:** lateral raise (isolation: dumbbell, cable, machine) and upright row (compound).
- **Posterior:** reverse fly and bent over lateral raise (isolation), prone high row (compound). Synergist for the latissimus dorsi in extension and adduction.

## 7. Pectoralis major

| Head | Origin | Fibres | Functions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clavicular (about 25 percent) | Medial half of the clavicle | Diagonal, downward | Horizontal adduction, internal rotation, flexion |
| Sternocostal | Sternum, top six costal cartilages | Horizontal | Horizontal adduction, internal rotation |
| Abdominal | External oblique aponeurosis | Diagonal, upward | Horizontal adduction, internal rotation |

- All three insert in the intertubercular groove. The sternocostal and abdominal heads together are about 75 percent.

## 8. Bench angles

- The bar path is a straight line. The direction the humerus points changes with the bench.
- **Incline +30:** clavicular head. **Flat:** sternocostal (abdominal second). **Decline -15:** abdominal (sternocostal second).
- Same trick for cable flies: high pulley for the abdominal head, mid pulley for the sternocostal head, low pulley for the clavicular head.
- Press means compound (with elbow extension). Fly means isolation. Incline scoop is a fly from a low pulley.

## 9. Pectoralis minor

- Ribs 3 to 5 to the coracoid process. Scapular protraction and scapular stabilisation. No exercise.

## 10. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Glenoid | A shallow cavity |
| Fossa | A pit or concave surface |
| Tubercle | A small bump |
| Tuberosity | A larger rough bump |
| Acromion | The highest point of the scapula |
| Coracoid | Crow-like (a beak-shaped process of the scapula) |
| Agonist and antagonist | A muscle and the one that does the opposite |

## 11. Where the sources differ

- **Anterior deltoid, vertical abduction:** the slide lists it. The class explained it as the muscle moving upward on the shoulder when the arm is externally rotated.
- **Groove name:** the slide says bicipital groove, the class said intertubercular groove (the same groove).

## 12. Revision questions

1. Why is the glenohumeral joint less stable than the hip?
2. Which joint action is possible at the shoulder but not at the hip?
3. Name the four rotator cuff muscles.
4. Which rotator cuff muscle does internal rotation?
5. Why can the supraspinatus only do the first 20 to 30 degrees of abduction?
6. When in the workout should the rotator cuff be strength trained, and why?
7. Why is a cable better than a dumbbell for rotation exercises?
8. Where does the anterior deltoid start?
9. What is the main function of the middle deltoid?
10. Which deltoid part does horizontal abduction?
11. What is the exceptional row?
12. What are the three heads of the pectoralis major?
13. Which head leads on a +30 degree incline bench?
14. Which head leads on a decline bench?
15. Where must the pulley be for an incline scoop?

## Answers

1. The socket is shallow, so the head of the humerus is only mounted on it.
2. Vertical abduction.
3. Supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor and subscapularis.
4. The subscapularis.
5. It only attaches near the tip of the humerus. The middle deltoid reaches further down and does the full movement.
6. At the end, because tired stabilisers would not hold the joint during heavy pressing.
7. The line of force matches the horizontal line of movement through the whole range.
8. On the lateral half of the clavicle.
9. Shoulder abduction.
10. The posterior deltoid.
11. The prone high row.
12. Clavicular, sternocostal and abdominal.
13. The clavicular head.
14. The abdominal head.
15. At the bottom (a low pulley).
